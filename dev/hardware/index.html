Hardware¶

An NTC thermistor and its divider as the driver uses them: the two terminals, the package families, the divider, the series resistor, the ADC input, self-heating and tolerances. Every number here is general or derived from the beta equation; the figures for one part are labelled "worked example" and come from a preset whose table rows are unverified (the vendor's sheet prints no table).

Datasheet sections this page relies on
  • Voltage divider and the series resistor: Public references n/a n/a, Divider, V = V_s R_n / (R_s + R_n), p. 1
  • Self-heating power: Public references n/a n/a, Dissipated power, P = V_s^2 R_n / (R_s + R_n)^2, p. 1
  • Example part: package and ratings: TDK none printed Rev.3, Dimension and Construction; Electrical Characteristics: power, dissipation, current, voltage, range, p. 1

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

The part¶

A thermistor has two terminals and no polarity. Its resistance falls as it heats: for most parts roughly 3 to 5 % per kelvin near room temperature. The driver names the terminals T1 and T2 so the figures can draw them; neither the part nor the driver cares which is which.

Families, as examples and typical, not a datasheet claim (the package drawn is the 0603 chip family; sizes are family nominals, check your part):

Common classes: R25 of 2.2, 4.7, 10, 47 or 100 kOhm; B of about 3000 to 4300 K; 1 to 5 % on R25 and 1 to 3 % on B; rated ranges of about -40 to +125 or +150 degrees Celsius, higher for glass. The divider¶

The driver reads the node of a resistor divider. In low-side form (the thermistor to ground) the node falls as the part heats; in high-side form (the thermistor to the supply) it rises.

Supply pins point up to their supply and decoupling, ground pins down. Pins on the right carry the loads and the pins tied to a supply or ground; a cross marks a pin left open. Export a circuit for a schematic or PCB tool: kokeb-docs hardware --kicad-netlist OUT.net --circuit divider.

With Rs the series resistor and Rn the thermistor, the node's share of the supply is Rn / (Rs + Rn) low side and Rs / (Rs + Rn) high side. Config::topology says which. If the ADC's reference is the divider's supply, the code is a pure ratio and the supply drops out (ratiometric, the default). If it is not, declare both adc_vref_mv and divider_supply_mv (see Configuration); declaring one without the other is InvalidArgument.

Choosing the series resistor¶

The node moves most where Rn equals Rs, so put that at the middle of the range you care about. A good rule for a whole range is the geometric mean:

Rs = sqrt(R(Tmin) * R(Tmax))

Worked example (rows unverified): 188.5 kOhm at -40 and 313 Ohm at +150 give 7.7 kOhm; 10 kOhm centres the divider at 25 degrees Celsius instead and is the common choice. Round to the nearest value you can buy and give the driver the real value in series_ohm (1 to 16 777 216 ohms), with its tolerance in series_tol_ppm so the bound includes it.

What an ADC step is worth¶

Near a divider's middle one ADC step is about T^2 / B / (r (1 - r)) / 2^bits kelvin, with r the node's share. It is finest where r is 0.5 and coarsens toward the ends. Worked example, a 12-bit ADC, 10 kOhm series resistor and the example preset (derived from its rows, which are unverified):

Temperature (degC) Resistance (ohm) Code One code is (K)
-35 144 300 3831 0.076
0 27 280 2997 0.028
25 10 000 2048 0.026
60 3 019 950 0.044
85 1 451 519 0.079
125 534 208 0.220
145 346 137 0.353

A 16-bit ADC divides these by 16. The driver carries resistance to 1/256 ohm inside, so its own rounding stays well below one code even at the hot end.

The ADC input¶

  • Source impedance. The divider's output impedance is Rs in parallel with Rn, up to Rs. Read your ADC's recommended source impedance; a high one needs a capacitor at the node, and averaging (sample_count) helps.
  • Filter and settling. 100 nF from the node to ground is a common start (a time constant of about 1 ms at 10 kOhm); the thermistor is slow, so a slow filter costs nothing but start-up time. The node settles with the time constant (Rs || Rn) x C, where Rn is the part's resistance at the cold end, so set settle_us to at least five of them (5 ms for 10 kOhm and 100 nF), and check that the ADC's sampling capacitor charges through the source impedance within its sampling time. A bias switch is needed for settle_us to mean anything: without one it is not allowed.
  • A cut wire. A broken wire between the node and the ADC pin leaves the input floating, and the driver cannot tell it from a temperature: a pull resistor at the pin, or a reading with the divider off, is yours.
  • Range. The node must stay inside the ADC's input range; with separate supply and reference, choose Rs so the node share times the supply stays below the reference.
  • Margins. A node within open_margin_codes of the open rail reads OpenCircuit, within short_margin_codes of the short rail ShortCircuit (2 codes by default, 1 or more with separate supply and reference). The margins are in codes: size them to the noise of the node, a few codes at 12 bits, hundreds at 20 bits and wider.

Self-heating¶

The divider heats the thermistor: P = Vs^2 * Rn / (Rs + Rn)^2, at most Vs^2 / (4 Rs). The rise is P divided by the part's dissipation constant in your mounting, in milliwatts per kelvin; about 1 mW/K is typical for a chip part on a board (the worked example's sheet gives 1 mW/degC at 25 degrees Celsius), but yours comes from its datasheet and your assembly. At 3.3 V with 10 kOhm and 10 kOhm that is 272 uW, about 0.27 K at 1 mW/K, which is ten ADC steps of the worked example. SelfHeatingMicrowatts() computes P; the driver does not correct for it. To cut it, power the divider from a GPIO only while sampling (SetBias(), see Porting), raise Rs, or lower the supply.

reference.cpp
// How much a 3.3 V divider warms the part: P in microwatts, divided by the
// part's dissipation constant (about 1 mW/K for a chip part on a board: read
// your datasheet).
uint32_t SelfHeatingMillikelvin(uint32_t r_ntc_ohm, uint32_t dissipation_uw_per_k) {
  const ntc_thermistor::Config c{.curve = ntc_thermistor::presets::kTdkNtcg163jf103ft1s,
                                 .adc_vref_mv = 3300,
                                 .divider_supply_mv = 3300};
  const uint32_t microwatts = ntc_thermistor::SelfHeatingMicrowatts(c, r_ntc_ohm);
  return (microwatts * 1000U) / dissipation_uw_per_k; // 272 uW at 25 degC and 1000 uW/K: 272 mK
}

Tolerance¶

At temperature T (kelvin) a relative error dR/R in the resistance is a temperature error of (T^2 / B) * (dR/R); at 25 degrees Celsius with B 3435 K, 1 % is 0.26 K. A B error matters away from the 25 degree point: it is multiplied by ln(R/R0). Reading::uncertainty_mc adds these terms, the series resistor's tolerance and one converter step.

Faults the divider cannot tell apart¶

A cut wire or missing part drives a low-side node to the supply rail: OpenCircuit. A shorted part drives it to ground: ShortCircuit. A divider whose supply has collapsed also reads as a short on a low-side divider (the node is at ground), and the driver has no second channel to tell them apart. If that matters, measure the supply on another channel in your application.

Adapting hardware.yml and the card to your part¶

docs/hardware.yml describes a generic two-terminal part. To describe yours:

  1. chip: the part's name.
  2. package: its type, the outline from the part's drawing and the variants from its ordering table.
  3. The ntc block's detail: R25 and B, or "table".
  4. The circuits: R1, C1 and the supply of your divider.
  5. Point each refs entry at your datasheet (document ds in docs/datasheets.yml: the section and the printed page) and delete each unverified you have confirmed.

Keep the pins T1 and T2 and the two hooks. Then run kokeb-docs hardware --check, kokeb-docs figures and kokeb-docs gen: the pinout, the outline, the block diagram, the circuits and the README's chip card follow.
